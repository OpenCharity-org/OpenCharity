DEDUCTIONS OF CHARITABLE CONTRIBUTIONS IN DEVELOPING COUNTRIES 
 
A Comprehensive Summary 
 
 
 
 
The definition of the term developing countries, as found in this study, is based on 
a classification of the United Nations shown in "Appendix B The United Nations Roster of 
'Developing' Countries" (see Appendix) published in Towards a World Economy that Works (1980). 
 
 
 
The present study is based on the 116 countries found in the "United Nations 
Roster" and deals with charitable contributions to be deducted by business enterprises as well as 
individual taxpayers or both simultaneously.  These developing countries fall into the following 
categories:  42 countries which expressly permit deductions, 37 countries which do not expressly 
permit deductions, 4 countries which prohibit deductions, and 33 countries for which updated 
information was not found in sources available in the collections of the Law Library of Congress. 
 
 
 
The calculation of deductions for charitable contributions in 13 countries is based 
on gross income, in 6 countries on taxable income, in 4 countries on net income, and in 19 countries 
on miscellaneous other conditions. 
 
 
 
The 42 countries which permit deductions for charitable contributions include 25 
countries which apply variable rates of deduction expressed in percentages, 10 countries which do 
not have rates established by law but by implementing regulations, and 10 countries which limit 
deductions to a certain amount. 
 
 
 
Detailed information for each country is available in the separate reports by 
individual countries or groups of countries. 
 
 
 
 
Prepared by Anton Wekerle, Chief 
Near Eastern and African Law Division 
Law Library of Congress 
May 1992

ARGENTINA 
 
 
              The Law on Income Tax* provides that charitable contributions are deductible if they are 
donated to religious, charitable, educational, scientific, cultural, artistic and sporting institutions. 
These gifts receive a tax credit up to a maximum of 20% of the taxpayer's total net income of the 
tax year. If the donation exceeds this maximum for a certain year, the remaining amount may be 
deducted within the following two taxable years, always keeping the 20% limitation in each year.

BANGLADESH 
 
 
 
 
No income tax is payable on the following income or donations of an assessee:1 
 
●  donations to an educational institution, including a polytechnic, which is affiliated to a 
university or a legally established board of education, recognized or run by the government; 
 
●  any hospital aided, run or recognized by the government or a local authority;  
 
●  any flood or famine relief fund sponsored or approved by the government; 
 
●  charitable contributions to an institution or fund for religious or charitable purposes that

●  contributions to the Zakat Fund; and 
 
●  donations to national sports organizations.2

FIJI 
 
 
 
 
The Income Tax Act, Laws of Fiji, ch. 201 (1985), exempts from taxation any 
interest payable on any loan to any institution, body or irrevocable trust of public character which 
is not a resident of Fiji and which has been established solely for the relief of poverty or distress 
of the public or for the advancement of religion or education. 
 
 
 
The Income Tax Act also allows as a deduction from income any donations or 
contributions made to the War Memorial, Anti-Tuberculosis Fund and the Fiji Visitors Bureau.

business income may deduct from their taxable income, contributions, gifts, donations made to

business income.

REPUBLIC OF KOREA 
  
 
 
 
Contributions to certain institutions, such as social welfare, cultural, educational, 
religious, and charitable organizations, are included in deductible expenses, within a prescribed 
limit.  The limit for such contributions is the sum of 10% of taxable income before deduction of 
any donation in this category plus 2% per year of paid-in capital not in excess of five billion won 
(about U.S.$6,501,951).  Any donation in excess of this limit can be applied to entertainment 
expenses.*

MALAYSIA 
 
 
 
 
The 1967 Income Tax Act of Malaysia, as amended from time to time up to 1990, 
in its section 44 relating to taxable income, provides in subsection (6) for a deduction of an amount 
equal to any gift of money made by the taxpayer in the basis year for that year to the central 
government, a state government, a local authority, or an institution or organization approved for 
the purposes of the section by the Director General of Inland Revenue. 
 
 
 
Institution for purposes of such deductions is defined as an institution in Malaysia 
that is not operated or conducted primarily for profit and that is: 
 
●  a hospital; 
 
●  a public or benevolent institution; 
 
●  a university or other educational institution; 
 
●  a public authority or society engaged solely in research or other work connected with 
the causes, prevention, or cure of disease in human beings; or 
 
●  a government-assisted institution engaged in socio-economic research. 
 
 
 
An organization for purposes of deductions from taxable income is defined as an 
organization in Malaysia that is not operated or conducted primarily for profit and that is: 
 
●  an organization established and maintained exclusively to administer and augment a 
public or private fund established or held for the sole purpose of the establishme nt, 
enlargement, or improvement of an institution or solely for the provision of a scholarship, 
exhibition, or prize for an individual for educational work, research work, or other similar 
work in an institution or in what would be an institution if it were in Malaysia; or 
 
●  an organization established and maintained exclusively to administer and augment a 
public fund established or held solely for the relief of distress among members of the public; 
or 
 
●  an organization established and maintained exclusively to administer and augment a 
fund established and held solely for the construction, improvement or maintenance of a 
building in Malaysia which: 
 
-- is intended to be used (and, when constructed, is used) exclusively for the 
purposes of religious worship or the advancement of religion; and 
 
-- is intended to be open (and, when constructed, is open) to any member of the 
public for those purposes; or 
 

34 
-- is an organization that maintains or assists in maintaining a zoo, museum, art 
gallery, or similar undertaking or is engaged or in connection with the promotion 
of culture or the arts; or 
 
-- is an organization engaged in or in connection with the conservation or protection 
of animals. 
 
 
 
In addition, the Income Tax (Deduction of Donations in Kind to Disaster Relief 
Funds) Rules, issued in 1971, provide that amounts equal to the value of donations in kind made 
to the National Disaster Relief Fund or a State Disaster Relief Fund may be deducted from the 
gross income of the donor, which must be income derived from a business.  In order to be eligible 
for such deductions, the donation must have been made out of the stock in trade of that business 
and the amount of the deduction in respect of such donation must not exceed the adjusted income 
for the basis period for that year of assessment.

PAKISTAN 
 
 
 
 
An assessee may make charitable contributions to a university, a board of education, 
or to any hospital. However, the donation should not exceed:* 
 
●  500,000 rupees (approximately $20,000) in the case of a corporation; and 
 
●  1,000,000 rupees (approximately $40,000) in any other case. 
 
 
 
For charitable contributions to a religious institution or fund, approved by the 
Central Board of Revenue, the amount must not exceed 500,000 rupees. 
 
 
 
In any case, the aggregate of donations shall not exceed: 
 
●  in the case of a public corporation, 5% of its total income; 
 
●  7 1/2 of 1% of the total income for a private company; and 
 
●  in other cases, 25% of the total income.

RWANDA 
 
 
 
 
The Rwanda Income Tax Law* considers that charitable contributions are 
deductible expenses from the net business income of corporations.  Donations made to charities of 
a religious, scientific or philanthropic nature, established in favor of the citizens of Rwanda may 
be deducted without limit of amount, provided that these charities are considered to meet the above 
conditions by the Minister of Finances. 
 
 
 
Concerning the taxation of individuals, the law makes no mention of such 
deductible expenses from the net taxable income of individuals.

SINGAPORE 
 
 
 
 
Under the provisions of the Income Tax Act as amended up to 1990, deductions 
may be taken for certain charitable contributions. 
 
 
 
Section 37, in Part VI of the Act, contains provisions on how the assessable income 
of a taxpayer is to be ascertained.  Subsection (l) lays down the general rule, which is that the 
assessable income of any person from all sources chargeable with a tax under this Act for the year 
of assessment is the remainder of his statutory income for that year after deductions allowed under 
this Part have been taken.  Statutory income is income accruing in or derived from Singapore or 
received in Singapore from outside Singapore in respect of items listed in section 10 of the Act. 
 
 
 
Subsection (2) of section 37 then lists the deductions that may be taken in 
ascertaining assessable income, under the heading of "Loss in trade, business, profession or 
vocation."  This list, set forth in paragraph (c) of subsection (2), contains the deduction "Gifts to 
approved institutions of a public character."  Paragraph (c) states that an amount in respect of gifts 
of money made by the taxpayer in the year preceding the year of assessment to the government or 
to any approved institution of a public character in Singapore may be deducted.  It then defines the 
term institution of a public character as meaning an institution or fund in Singapore which is: 
 
●  a hospital not operated or conducted for profit; 
 
●  a public or benevolent institution not operated or conducted for profit; 
 
●  a public authority or society not operated or conducted for profit and which is engaged 
in research or other work connected with the causes, prevention, or cure of disease in 
human beings, where the gift is for such activities; 
 
●  a university or a public fund for the establishment, maintenance, enlargement, or 
improvement of a university; 
 
●  an educational institution not operated or conducted for profit, or a public fund for the 
establishment, maintenance, enlargement, or improvement of such an educational 
institution; 
 
●  a public or private fund for the provision, establishment, or endowment of a scholarship, 
exhibition, or prize in a university, or an educational institution not operated or conducted 
for profit; 
 
●  a public fund established and maintained for the relief of distress among members of the 
public; 
 
●  a charitable institution or a body of persons or a trust established for charitable purposes 
only; or 

46 
●  an organization not operated or conducted primarily for profit which is engaged in or 
connected with the promotion of culture or the arts or with the promotion of sports. 
 
 
 
Under paragraph (d) of subsection (2) of section 37, an amount equivalent to the 
value of any gift of a computer (including computer software and peripherals) approved by the 
Minister and made by any company in the year preceding the year of assessment to a prescribed 
education or research institution in Singapore may also be deducted. 
 
 
 
Subsection (2A) of section 37 stipulates, however, that deductions under this 
section will be allowed only to the extent that they are not in excess of the statutory income 
remaining after deductions authorized by subsection (2), paragraph (a).

THAILAND 
 
 
 
 
Income tax in Thailand is governed by the Revenue Code of 1939.  In accordance 
with amended section 47(7) of the Code,1 donations given to qualified charitable organizations are 
deductible up to a maximum of 10% of the income remaining after allowing the deductions listed 
in subparagraphs (1)-(6).  The allowed deductions listed in 47(1)(6) are: 
 
●  personal allowances for the taxpayer, his or her spouse and children; 
 
●  life insurance premiums; 
 
●  dividends received from mutual funds issued by financial institutions created 
specifically by law in order to promote agriculture, commerce, or industry; 
 
●  an educational allowance for children; 
 
●  an allowance for employee contributions to a provident fund; and 
 
●  an allowance for interest on loans for residential purposes, and so on. 
 
 
 
The tax allowance for donations can be equal to the amount donated. Charitable 
organizations approved by law include: 
 
●  government-owned clinics and educational institutions; 
 
●  public charities; and 
 
●  other clinics and educational institutions as specified by ministerial notifications 
published in the Government Gazette.2

ZAMBIA 
 
 
 
 
Income tax in Zambia is levied on both companies and individuals under the 
Income Tax Act, 1966, as amended (see 2 African Tax Systems, Supp. No. 77 of November, 1990). 
 
 
 
The following items are allowed as deductions: contributions in cash to approved 
ecclesiastical, charitable, research, or educational institutions or funds of a public character or to a 
national amateur sporting association (where no consideration is made for the payments).  The 
deductions may not exceed 15% of the assessable income.
